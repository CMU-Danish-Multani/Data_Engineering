Stock Intelligence for AI Infrastructure: architecture and schemas

How data on Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and NVIDIA moves from public sources into two databases, a dashboard and a question answering assistant: what each dataset is, how often it changes, which KPIs it feeds, and every table and column the team will build.

Mohd Danish Multani · 2026-11-13

Scope. Five AI infrastructure companies only: Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta and NVIDIA. Banking is out of scope.

Guiding rule for what is kept. The 10 KPIs per company are the core of the project and are all kept. Data is cut only where it feeds no KPI and no research question, or where it would bury the useful parts. Every cut is listed in section 2 and can be reversed.

1. At a glance

Think of it like a restaurant. Groceries arrive from different suppliers (the sources), are stored exactly as delivered (raw), are washed and chopped (cleaning), are prepared into dishes (serving tables), and are served to the customer (the dashboard and the assistant).

6 public datasetsSEC, prices, Census, calls

Servingstar schema + vectors

NoSQL · document database

MongoDB

  • SEC financial data (XBRL JSON), one document per company
  • 10-K and 10-Q reports, and 8-K earnings press releases, as received
  • Earnings call transcripts, one document per call
  • Raw only. Nothing cleaned is stored here.

Why: the shape varies from record to record, and the content is semi-structured or unstructured.

SQL · relational database

PostgreSQL

  • Raw tables: 13F holdings, daily prices, Census construction
  • Staging: SQL views that clean, plus one table rebuilt each run
  • Core star schema with all KPI values
  • Mart views, one per dashboard need
  • Run log and quality log

Why: the data is rows and columns, SQL is best at joining, and the course requires structured data here.

Vector search · inside PostgreSQL (pgvector)

Text chunks + embeddings

  • Management discussion sections, earnings press releases and calls, cut into chunks of about 500 words
  • Each tagged with company, document, section and filing number
  • Each chunk's embedding: a list of numbers capturing its meaning

Why inside PostgreSQL: a few thousand chunks is small, and one SQL query can fetch passages and KPIs together.

SEC XBRL financials quarterly

MongoDB raw_xbrl · source: data.sec.gov company facts API, one JSON file per company
What it is
Every number a company has ever tagged in its filings, in one file. Think of a giant spreadsheet where each row is a labelled item ("Revenues", "Cash", "Inventory") with a value for every quarter since about 2009. Each value carries its dates, the filing it came from and when that was filed.
Size
Several hundred labels (called tags) per company. I have not counted per company.
What we use
About 6 tags: revenue, operating income, operating cash flow, capital expenditure, depreciation, and segment revenue where tagged.
Impact
The backbone of Question 1 and every KPI page. Without it there are no standard KPIs.
Left out
Raw file kept whole (course rule: preserve raw). Only the mapped tags are pulled into staging. The user never sees the other tags.

10-K and 10-Q reports yearly and quarterly

MongoDB raw_filings · source: SEC EDGAR archives
What it is
The official annual (10-K) and quarterly (10-Q) reports. A 10-K runs to roughly 80 to 150 pages, mostly legal and standard text. Rough, from general knowledge.
Useful part
Management's Discussion and Analysis (MD&A): 10-K Item 7 and 10-Q Part I Item 2. This is where management explains results, spending and AI plans in its own words.
Impact
Question 3 (do words match numbers) and the assistant's answers.
Left out
Not chunked or embedded: Business description (barely changes), Risk Factors (long and mostly boilerplate), financial statement text (we already have the numbers from XBRL), and all exhibits. The raw HTML is still stored whole; only MD&A goes on to the vector table, which keeps search results focused.

8-K earnings releases quarterly

MongoDB raw_filings · source: SEC EDGAR archives
What it is
An 8-K is a notice of an important event, filed at any time. Only one kind matters to us: Item 2.02 "Results of Operations", the quarterly earnings announcement. Its press release (exhibit 99.1) is where numbers like Azure growth or AWS revenue appear.
Impact
The source for product KPIs that XBRL does not carry, and quotable management statements for the assistant.
Left out
Not downloaded at all: every other kind of 8-K (director changes, bond issues, shareholder votes) and every exhibit other than 99.1. This removes most 8-K traffic.

Earnings call transcripts quarterly · loaded once

MongoDB raw_calls · source: public dataset on Hugging Face
What it is
The text of each quarterly call: about 20 minutes of prepared remarks from executives, then analysts' questions and answers.
Impact
Question 3 and the assistant. The question and answer part is the most valuable, because analysts press management on capex and AI returns.
Left out
Nothing. Roughly 28 calls per company over 7 years. Because the dataset is a fixed snapshot, the most recent quarters may be missing; coverage is measured per company and quarter.

13F fund holdings quarterly

PostgreSQL raw.f13_* · source: SEC Form 13F data sets
What it is
Every quarter, every fund managing over $100 million lists every stock it holds. The file covers all funds and all stocks, which is why the latest quarterly zip is about 96 MB.
What we use
Rows for our 5 companies (who bought or sold), and each fund's total size (to pick the top 25 funds).
Impact
Question 2 and the Smart Money page.
Left out
History before 2023 is not loaded. Question 2 asks about the last four quarters; loading from the first quarter of 2023 gives about 15 quarters of trend and more than halves the largest dataset. It also avoids the old thousands of dollars unit. Each loaded quarter is kept in full in raw (it counts toward the course's volume requirement); filtering to our 5 companies happens in staging. The three 13F tables we do not need (other managers, signatures) are not loaded.

Daily stock prices daily

PostgreSQL raw.prices_daily · source: Tiingo API (proposed, to be confirmed)
What it is
One row per company per trading day: open, high, low, close, adjusted close and volume. "Adjusted" means corrected for stock splits, so a 10 for 1 split does not look like a 90% crash.
Impact
Stock return against peers (Question 1), the 13F implied price check, and earnings day reaction.
Left out
Raw keeps what the API sends. Only close and adjusted close go on to core; open, high, low and volume are not used.
Live price
A "current price" card on the dashboard may call a free API when the page opens. It is display only, never stored, and nothing depends on it.

Census data center construction monthly

PostgreSQL raw.census_vip · source: U.S. Census Bureau construction spending
What it is
Monthly U.S. spending on building data centers. A single series. Each release also revises the last few months.
Impact
Answers "does company spending track national data center construction" in Question 1.
Left out
Nothing. All releases are kept so revisions can be shown.

Removed: FRED interest rates

previously PostgreSQL raw.fred_obs
Why removed
Rates mattered for banks, where they drive lending margins. With banks dropped, rates gave only loose background for the AI companies and fed no KPI. The course requires every dataset to contribute meaningfully (requirement 9), so a weak one is a risk rather than a bonus.
Bring back if
The team wants a specific question such as "did higher borrowing costs slow capex growth".

4. End to end pipeline

Data moves left to right. Everything inside the dashed blue outline is one PostgreSQL database. The two purple rails underneath are shared by every step.

raw, kept exactly as received cleaning and text processing serving tables vector search shared rules and run metadata dashed box: optional source

The pipeline, step by step

  1. Setup, onceCreate the MongoDB database stockintel_raw and the PostgreSQL database stockintel with six schemas: raw, staging, core, mart, ref, meta. Enable the vector extension. Seed core.dim_company (5 rows), core.dim_metric (the KPIs in section 3), core.dim_series, ref.xbrl_tag_map and ref.text_term from a reviewed CSV.
  2. ExtractEach job asks its source only for records newer than the watermark in its last successful meta.load_run row. First run: backfill fiscal years 2019 to 2025 (13F from the first quarter of 2023).
  3. ValidateBefore writing anything: is the file non empty, are the required fields present, does it parse. A failure marks the run failed, writes a critical dq_issue, and stops. Nothing partial is written.
  4. Land rawWrite exactly what arrived: JSON and HTML to MongoDB, tabular files to raw.* tables with every source column as text. Upsert on the natural key so reruns never duplicate.
  5. StageA MongoDB aggregation flattens XBRL JSON into rows, written to staging.xbrl_fact (emptied and rebuilt each run). Every other cleaning rule is a SQL view, staging.v_*. Each rule that changes or drops rows logs the count to meta.dq_issue.
  6. Load coreUpsert from staging into the core star schema, where real types, keys, foreign keys and check constraints apply. A constraint failure is a critical issue.
  7. Process textFor each new raw filing or call: add a core.dim_document row, keep only MD&A (filings), the press release (8-K) or the call, cut chunks, count terms, embed, insert into core.doc_chunk. Product KPIs read from press releases go to core.fact_kpi with the filing number.
  8. CheckRun the checks in section 10. Any failure is logged; critical ones mark the run failed.
  9. Servemart.v_* views compute growth, margins, free cash flow, ranks and joins on read. The dashboard and the assistant read only mart and core, never raw.

6. MongoDB schema

Type: NoSQL document database. A document is like a JSON file that can hold nested lists, and two documents in one collection may have different fields. Hosting: MongoDB Atlas free tier, so all four members can reach it. Database: stockintel_raw. Raw data only.

Every document carries three load fields: run_id (matches meta.load_run), fetched_at, source_url. A light validator requires only these and _id, so no source record is rejected.

raw_xbrl changes quarterly

One document per company per version of the SEC company facts file. The file is cumulative (it holds every past filing), so a new document is inserted only when its content hash changes, about four times a year per company.

7. Vector store and RAG

Type: vector index inside PostgreSQL, using the pgvector extension. Not a separate database. An embedding is a list of numbers that captures what a passage means, so two passages about the same idea have similar lists even with different words. The database finds the closest lists to a question's list.

What goes in: only three kinds of text. MD&A sections of 10-K and 10-Q reports, 8-K earnings press releases, and earnings calls. Risk Factors, business descriptions and exhibits are left out so search results are not buried in boilerplate.

core.doc_chunk

Grain: one chunk of one section of one document. Rough size after the cuts: about 6,000 to 7,000 rows. Plausible, rough estimate (about 3 million words / 500), not yet counted.

ColumnTypeRule
chunk_idbigserialprimary key
document_idbigintforeign key to dim_document (company, date, filing number come from there)
sectiontextcheck in ('mdna', 'press_release', 'prepared_remarks', 'qa')
speaker_roletextexecutive, analyst, operator; null for filings
chunk_indexintegerorder within the section; unique (document_id, section, chunk_index)
chunk_texttextabout 500 words, HTML removed
word_countinteger
ai_term_count, capex_term_countintegermatches of ref.text_term, summed by v_text_signal
embeddingvector(d)d set by the model: 384 for all-MiniLM-L6-v2, 1536 for OpenAI text-embedding-3-small
embed_run_idintegerforeign key to meta.embed_run

Index: HNSW on embedding vector_cosine_ops. HNSW is a structure that finds near neighbours quickly without comparing the question to every chunk.

How the assistant answers

question: "How did Microsoft explain its capex jump in fiscal 2025?"
  1. embed the question with the same model as embed_run
  2. SELECT chunk_text, accession_no, section
       FROM core.doc_chunk c JOIN core.dim_document d USING (document_id)
      WHERE d.company_id = MSFT AND d.period in FY2025
      ORDER BY c.embedding <=> :question_embedding     -- cosine distance
      LIMIT 8;
  3. SELECT capex, revenue, free_cash_flow FROM mart.v_kpi_annual WHERE MSFT, FY2025
  4. send passages + numbers to the language model
  5. answer cites each filing number or call date it used

The rule that makes it trustworthy: numbers come from the checked SQL tables, never from the text.
